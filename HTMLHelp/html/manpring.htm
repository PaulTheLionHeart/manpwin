Rings Fractals

ManpWIN 4.06a includes sixteen Rings subtypes, offering an experimental collection of complex rational maps. Some can produce ring-like structures, while others reveal root basins, branching boundaries, nested circles or chains of repeated forms. The name Rings describes this collection; it does not mean every subtype produces a mathematical Herman ring.

Select Rings from the Fractal Type dialogue (press T). Choose the subtype and enter its parameters in the fractal parameter dialogue. Each subtype has different mathematical parameters and its own starting view. A change in parameter values may produce a very different image.

Selected Iteration Formulas

The following examples show some of the rational maps used by Rings. Here z is the current complex orbit value and the adjustable coefficients may be real or complex, as indicated in the parameter dialogue.

Newton Rational (subtype 9)

z(n+1) = z(n) - (z(n)^n - 1) / (n * z(n)^(n-1))

The degree n selects the number of roots of unity.

Basilica-Rabbit Rational (subtype 10)

z(n+1) = (z(n)^2 - a) / (z(n)^2 - 1)

The parameter a is complex; the denominator has poles at z = +1 and -1.

Bicritical Rational (subtype 11)

z(n+1) = (z(n)^n + a) / (b * z(n)^n + 1)

The degree n and complex parameters a and b control the map.

Chebyshev-Halley Rational (subtype 12)

f(z) = z^n - 1
L(z) = f(z) * f''(z) / (f'(z) * f'(z))
z(n+1) = z(n) - (1 + L(z)/(2 * (1 - alpha * L(z)))) * f(z)/f'(z)

The signed parameter alpha selects different methods: 0 is Chebyshev, 0.5 is Halley and 1 is Super-Halley. Negative values are also available for experimentation.

Joukowski Rational (subtype 13)

z(n+1) = a * (z(n) + 1/z(n)) + b

The coefficients a and b are complex; zero is a pole.

Sierpinski Rational (subtype 14)

z(n+1) = a * (z(n) + 1/z(n)^2) + b

This is an experimental map with a pole at zero. Its subtype name is descriptive rather than a guarantee of Sierpinski geometry.

Perturbed Basilica (subtype 15)

z(n+1) = z(n)^2 - 1 + lambda/z(n)^2

The perturbation lambda is complex. At lambda = 0 the formula reduces to the Basilica polynomial; nonzero lambda introduces a pole at zero. Both positive and negative lambda values can produce interesting results.

Colouring Options

Each Rings subtype provides a Colouring parameter. The available values are:

ValueModeHow it works
0NormalUses the ordinary Rings iteration colouring.
1Orbit SumUses the magnitude of the accumulated orbit positions to select a palette colour.
2Orbit Sum ContoursIntroduces narrow contour lines into orbit-sum colouring.
3Orbit TrapTracks the minimum squared distance of the orbit from the origin.
4ConvergenceTracks the minimum squared distance between consecutive orbit points.

For orbit-trap and convergence modes, small distances are mapped logarithmically into palette colours. These measurements can reveal structures that are not obvious under normal colouring. They are colouring methods, not changes to the mathematical formula.

Four Corner Rendering

The separate Four Corner parameter enables an experimental orbit-separation renderer. Rather than following one orbit per pixel, ManpWIN follows four nearby starting points and records the largest separation reached between their orbits during iteration. This can highlight fine boundaries, repeating loops and interior structures.

Four Corner is distinct from the Colouring parameter. It may take considerably longer to render than the ordinary method, particularly with higher arithmetic precision. Colouring combinations may behave differently in the Four Corner path because this method uses a separate orbit calculation.

Arithmetic and Limitations

Rings supports ordinary double, double-double (DD) and quad-double (QD) calculation paths. The specialised orbit-sum, orbit-trap and convergence colouring has been implemented for these three precisions. Some Rings operations do not have corresponding MPFR implementations; do not assume that every experimental colouring or rendering combination is available at arbitrary precision.

Rational maps may have poles where a denominator becomes zero. Near such values, the orbit may grow very rapidly or terminate. Default values are starting points only; users are encouraged to experiment with parameters, colour palettes, iterations and zoom levels.

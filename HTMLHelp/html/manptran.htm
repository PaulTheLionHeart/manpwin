Fractal Transformations

Overview

Fractal transformations modify the coordinates used when calculating a fractal. They can be used to rotate, fold, stretch, distort, remap, or otherwise alter the geometry of the image.

Press Ctrl+X to open the Fractal Transformations dialog.

Transformations are separate from the normal fractal formula. This makes it possible to apply the same transformation to different supported fractals.

Coordinate Systems

The transformation dialog provides several coordinate systems:

  • Cartesian
  • Spherical
  • Cylindrical
  • Parabolic
  • Paraboloidal
  • Elliptical
  • Bipolar
  • Toroidal
  • Prolate Spheroidal
  • Oblate Spheroidal
  • Bispherical
  • Conical

These change the coordinate system used when mapping points and can produce substantially different forms from the same underlying fractal.

View Rotation

The current fractal view may be displayed at 0, 90, 180 or 270 degrees, or at an arbitrary angle.

Rotation: The rotation angle is absolute relative to the standard unrotated fractal view. Rotations are not cumulative. For example, changing the rotation from 30° to 45° produces a view rotated 45° from the standard orientation, not an additional 45° rotation from the current view.

Arbitrary-angle rotation is performed about the centre of the current view.

Standard Transformations

The following transformations are available:

  • Inversion
  • Complex Logarithm
  • Fold X
  • Fold Y
  • Fold XY
  • Fold Diagonal
  • Fold Diagonals
  • Radial Bulge
  • Radial Pinch
  • Circle Fold
  • Radial Clamp
  • Radial Compress
  • Complex Exponential
  • Complex Sine
  • Power

Many of these transformations use a centre position and radius to determine where the effect is applied.

Power Transformation

The Power transformation raises the transformed coordinate to a selected integer power.

The power must be an integer of 2 or greater.

Higher powers can produce increasingly strong rotational symmetry and distortion.

Extended Transformations

Several transformations use their own specialised parameters rather than the normal centre and radius controls.

Affine

Affine transformations provide precise control over scaling, stretching, shearing, reflection, rotation and translation using six coefficients. Because these parameters can be combined in many different ways, Affine transformations are described separately.

See Affine Transformations

Möbius

The Möbius transformation provides a complex-plane mapping controlled by its own set of parameters.

Joukowski

The Joukowski transformation provides another specialised complex-plane mapping with its own parameters.

Twist

The Twist transformation applies a specialised distortion controlled by its own parameters.

Choosing a Transformation

Different transformations can produce very different results depending on the fractal, location and scale being viewed. Simple transformations such as folds and radial effects are useful for quickly experimenting with symmetry and shape.

Affine transformations are particularly useful when precise geometric control is required, such as stretching by a known amount, applying a known shear angle, or rotating and translating coordinates in combination.
